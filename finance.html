Start with the basics

Ways to put money to work.

Investing can help you work toward longer-term goals. Compare risk, time horizon, fees, and access to your money before choosing an approach.

Ownership · Market risk

Stocks & stock funds

Individual stocks represent ownership in a company. Index funds and ETFs can spread investments across many companies, though their value can still fall.

Explore stock investing ↗

Lending · Interest-rate risk

Bonds

Treasury securities, municipal bonds, corporate bonds, and bond funds offer different income, tax, credit, and interest-rate considerations. Bond values can decline, especially as rates change.

Understand bond types ↗

Property · Liquidity risk

Real estate & REITs

Property can provide rental income and potential appreciation, while real estate investment trusts offer another way to invest in property markets. Costs, vacancies, debt, and market declines matter.

Learn about REITs ↗

Cash alternatives · Lower volatility

Savings, CDs & Treasury bills

High-yield savings accounts, certificates of deposit, and short-term Treasury bills may suit near-term goals. Terms, liquidity, insurance coverage, and inflation can affect results.

Treasury bill basics ↗

Pooled investments

Mutual funds & ETFs

Funds can hold stocks, bonds, or other assets. Compare diversification, underlying holdings, costs, and risks; diversification does not guarantee against losses.

Compare funds ↗

Speculative · High volatility

Digital assets

Bitcoin and other cryptoassets can experience sharp losses and may have limited investor protections. Consider these risks carefully before committing money.

Cryptoasset basics ↗

Examples are educational, not personalized recommendations. All investments involve risk; goals and circumstances differ. Roth IRAs and 401(k)s are account types that may hold various investments.
